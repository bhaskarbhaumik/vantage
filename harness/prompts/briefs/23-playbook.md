## §20.1 Quick Wins (0–6 months)

**Each quick win is tied to an evidenced pain point AND a named `P-nn`.** Both. A quick win with no
named stakeholder is a proposal with nobody to receive it; one with no evidenced pain is a solution
looking for a problem.

Quick means: deliverable inside a budget cycle, not requiring a new procurement vehicle, and
addressing something the client has already said is a problem.

## §20.2 Medium-Term Growth (6–18 months)

`O-nn` reference per row. Sequenced from the register by attractiveness and evidenced timing, not by
service-line preference.

## §20.3 Strategic Plays (18+ months)

`O-nn` reference per row. These depend on relationship state that does not yet exist — say what has
to become true first, and make that a dependency rather than an assumption.

## §20.4 Relationship-Building Priorities

`P-nn` reference per row. Which relationships to build, in what order, and why that order.

Accessibility scores above 3 required account-team input (§10.6, Model 6). Where the Input Sheet is
absent, the priority order is based on **influence alone**, and you should say so — because an
influence-ranked list with unknown accessibility is a different instrument from a true
influence/accessibility grid.

## §20.5 Marketing Messages & Content Gaps

What content exists versus what this pursuit needs, tied to the §19.4 themes. Content gaps are
actionable; message invention is `account-strategy`'s lane.

## §20.6 Next Steps & Action Items — the `A-nn` register

`T-20.6` + `C-20` + `F-20`. **7–12 actions**, each with:

`A-nn` · action · **owner role** (a role, not a person's name unless the account team supplied one)
· **due date** · **success metric** · horizon · refs (`O-nn` / `P-nn` / `G-nnnn` / `C-` IDs).

**A success metric is what makes an action reviewable.** "Meet the CIO" is not an action; "Secure a
45-minute CIO discovery session on the core-modernization decision by 2026-11-15; success = a
follow-on technical workshop scheduled" is.

### The coverage rule — mechanically checked (V7e)

**Every `HYPOTHESIS` and `VALIDATE` opportunity from §18, and every `materiality: high` gap from the
merged gap register, must appear as an action here or as a §19.9 validation item.**

Read `account-strategy`'s handoff to see what §19.9 already covers, then cover the remainder.
Neither of you covering an item is the failure mode; both covering it is merely untidy.

For each, the action is the **validation step**: what would establish it, who would do that, by
when. A high-materiality gap with no validation action is a known unknown that nobody is closing.

## Counterweight to structural optimism

Tier 3 is structurally optimistic — every section from §18 onward exists to argue for investment.
Two things in your remit push back, and you must not soften them:

1. **Dated actions with success metrics make the thesis falsifiable.** A metric that cannot fail is
   not a metric.
2. **State what would mean stopping.** Where the thesis rests on an assumption that could be
   disproved — an incumbent renewal, a budget deferral, a leadership change — name the observable
   signal, the source to monitor, and the decision if it triggers. Fold this into §20.3 or as a
   closing note in §20.6.

## Traps

- **Do not write positioning.** That is §19.2–19.4.
- **Do not name an organization individual.** Owner roles, unless the Input Sheet supplied names.
- **Do not date an action against an invented client event.** Timing comes from §18.6, which comes
  from evidenced events (V9d).
- **Do not produce twelve actions where seven are real.** The register's value is that every line is
  owned and dated; padding it destroys that.
- **An action referencing a refuted claim is a §18 problem** — check the register status before
  building on it.
