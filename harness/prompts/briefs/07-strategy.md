# Brief 07 — `strategy` · Wave A · Block 0700–0799 · Owns §9 Strategic Priorities & Transformation Agenda

You capture **what this company says it is doing, and whether it is actually doing it**. §9.6 is the
credibility check that makes the rest of the section trustworthy — announced-versus-delivered is
where most transformation narratives quietly fall apart.

§9.1's quoted priorities are the test every §19.4 message is measured against, so quote precisely.

Output: `sections/09-strategy.md` · fragment `state/fragments/strategy.json`.

## §9.1 Stated Corporate Strategy & Priorities

5–7 priorities, **each with a verbatim quote, its date, its venue, and its transcript provenance
class** (`official` / `third-party` / `machine`). `T-9.1`.

These come from the company's own words: annual-report strategy sections, CEO letters, investor-day
material, earnings calls. A priority you paraphrase from a journalist's summary is a Tier-4 claim
about a Tier-1 fact — go get the original.

Sequence them as the company sequences them. The order management uses is itself evidence.

## §9.2 Active Transformation Programs

`T-9.2` + `F-9.2`. Named programmes with status, disclosed budget and timeline, partners, and
incumbents where evidenced.

**Evidence status per programme, and it matters more here than anywhere else.** A programme that
was announced is `ANNOUNCED`. Budget and timeline appear only where CONFIRMED or ANNOUNCED, or as
`[ESTIMATED]` with the basis shown. **Never invent a budget from programme scope.**

This is the section whose claims most often become §18 opportunities. An `ANNOUNCED` programme that
reads as an in-flight one produces an opportunity built on something that may never have started —
which is precisely the chain `evidence-audit` exists to break.

## §9.3 M&A Strategy & Recent Transactions

**9.3.1** Acquisitions and divestitures over three years: value, rationale, integration state.
**9.3.2** Pipeline and stated intent — **`ANNOUNCED` or `OBSERVED-SIGNAL` only. No invented deals,
no rumoured targets presented as pipeline.** A banker's speculation in the trade press is a Tier-4
`OBSERVED-SIGNAL` at `LOW`, if it appears at all.

## §9.4 Innovation & R&D Focus

R&D spend and intensity trend (from the income statement), labs and innovation centres, corporate
venture arms and notable bets, university and startup partnerships. **Empty is a valid render.**

## §9.5 Strategic Partnerships & Alliances

Named alliances and what they signal about direction. `vendors` owns the commercial vendor
relationship view in §16 — you own the strategic-intent read. Hand off overlaps.

## §9.6 Transformation Outcomes & Benefits Realization

**The credibility check on §9.2.** Announced milestones versus delivered ones, KPIs the company
committed to versus what it reported, adoption evidence, and — importantly — **failure and reset
signals**: quietly dropped programmes, extended timelines, leadership changes on a programme,
restated targets.

**Every delivered-outcome claim requires independent corroboration** (`ind×1`). A company reporting
that its own transformation succeeded is `ANNOUNCED`.

This subsection is often thin, and thin is the honest answer: most companies announce loudly and
report outcomes quietly. Say so, with the search record, rather than treating announcement volume
as delivery evidence.

## Traps

- **Strategy-page language is marketing.** The strategy section of an annual report and a
  CEO letter carry weight; a "Our Strategy" web page usually does not.
- **A priority repeated across four quarters is one priority**, not four data points — but the
  repetition itself is evidence of durability, and worth stating.
- **Do not build a programme register from job postings.** Hiring for a capability is
  `OBSERVED-SIGNAL` and belongs to `signals` in §13.3.
- **Deal values are disclosed or absent.** Never estimated (V9d).
- **Do not describe integration as complete** because the deal closed.
